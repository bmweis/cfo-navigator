Boat — Before vs. After

Left: the flat seafoam sail from earlier passes—low contrast against a seafoam-tinted sky/water. Right: white/cream sailcloth with a navy edge trim, subtle fold shading, rigging lines, and a bowsprit—reads as an actual sail at a glance, and ties back to the palette through the trim rather than the whole sail.

Before
After
Sail changes: white/cream cloth instead of solid seafoam (matches how real sails actually look, and fixes the contrast problem against a light sky/water background), navy stroke around every sail edge so it stays crisp regardless of what's behind it, two faint fold lines for fabric volume, a thin seafoam trim on the leech to keep a brand touch without recoloring the whole sail. Hull changes: top-to-bottom gradient for dimension, a small cabin with a porthole, rigging lines, a bowsprit. Difficulty pills: two-tone diagonal gradient plus a glossy top highlight, same four-tier color logic as before (light/calm to dark/severe) but reads more like an actual button now instead of a flat swatch.
